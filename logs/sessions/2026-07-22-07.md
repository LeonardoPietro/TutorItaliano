## Session focus: Stage 00 topic — Definite articles (masculine singular)

### WHY
In Portuguese "o/a" barely takes thought. In Italian the definite article changes based on gender, number, AND the first letter/sound of the following word — without it, full sentences ("o livro") can't be formed.

### EXPLAIN
Scoped to masculine singular only (three forms):
1. **il** — default rule for most masculine words: il treno, il cane
2. **lo** — before S+consonant or Z: lo studente, lo zio
3. **l'** — before a vowel or H: l'amico, l'orologio, l'hotel

### SHOW / CHECK
Worked example given: "treno" → **il treno** (regular consonant start).
Three unaided checks, all correct on first try:
- orologio (vowel start) → **l'orologio** ✅
- zio (starts with Z) → **lo zio** ✅
- cane (regular consonant) → **il cane** ✅

### REFLECT
Asked the learner to explain in their own words how to choose between il/lo/l'.
Answer: "il é a regra comum / lo é para z e s+consoante / l' é para vogais e H"
Learner correctly recalled all three rules AND added the H exception unprompted — H had not been explicitly taught yet, but the learner generalized correctly (words with H behave as if they started with the following vowel sound). Confirmed correct, no re-teaching needed.
