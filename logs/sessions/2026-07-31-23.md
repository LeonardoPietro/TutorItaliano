## Concepts Taught

### 1. **Negation (Negação)**
   - **Passo 1:** Basic negation — *non* is obligatory before the verb (non parlo = I don't speak), cannot be omitted or appear at sentence end like in Portuguese
   - **Passo 2:** Other negation words: *niente* (nothing), *nessuno* (no one), both require *non* + verb pattern
   - **Pattern:** `non + verbo + [niente|nessuno]` (e.g., non vedo nessuno = I see no one)
   - **Key difference from Portuguese:** No flexibility — *non* always required upfront
   - **Example from Maneskin's "Voglio Volare":** "Io non ho niente da dire" (I have nothing to say) — perfect illustration of SM-2 pattern
   - **Check:** Learner correctly identified *nessuno* for "quem você vê?" (who do you see?) ✓
   - **Practice:** Learner correctly built "Non ho niente da fare" (I have nothing to do) unaided ✓

### 2. **Yes/No & Wh-Questions (Perguntas)**
   - **Yes/No questions:** Same word order as statement, but intonation rises at end (Tu parli italiano? ≠ Do you speak Italian? — no inversion unlike Portuguese)
   - **Wh-questions:** Interrogative (dove, quando, chi, cosa, come) moves to sentence-initial position
   - **Pattern for wh:** `[interrogativo] + verbo + [sujeito implicito ou explicito] + resto`
   - **Examples:** "Tu parli?" (yes/no) vs. "Dove parli?" (wh — where do you speak?) vs. "Chi conosci a Roma?" (who do you know in Rome?)
   - **Check 1:** Learner correctly predicted wh-question structure ("Che ore sono?" — what time is it?) ✓
   - **Check 2:** Learner correctly answered "Tu vieni domani?" (You come tomorrow?) as yes/no question structure ✓
   - **Practice:** Learner built "Sì, mangio pizza" (Yes, I eat pizza) correctly as complete response ✓
   - **Reflection:** Learner correctly identified the key difference: wh-questions move the interrogative to the start ✓

### 3. **Days, Months & Hours (Tempo)**
   - **Days (lunedì–domenica):** Always take article (il lunedì, la domenica, but optional when used as adverb)
   - **Months (gennaio–dicembre):** Generally NO article (Parto a luglio = I leave in July)
   - **Hours:** 
     - "Che ore sono?" (What time is it? — plural, most common)
     - Structure: `Sono le [num] + [period]` where period = di mattina (morning), del pomeriggio (afternoon), di sera (evening)
     - Exception: 1 hour = "È l'una" (singular), then "Sono le due" (plural from 2 onwards)
   - **Learner's attempt:** Said "sono le quindici" (15:00 in 24-hour format) — technically correct but formally/militarily styled; corrected to natural form "sono le tre del pomeriggio" (3 PM)
   - **Practice:** Learner correctly answered "Sono le dieci" (It's 10 o'clock) ✓
   - **Reflection:** Learner correctly identified "Che ore sono?" as the proper phrasing ✓
