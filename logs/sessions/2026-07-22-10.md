## Topic: Indefinite articles (un/uno/una/un')

Applied the standing completeness rule from Session 9 feedback: taught the full trigger set for both genders in one pass, not staged.

### WHY
Same phonetic-trigger logic as definite articles (il/lo/l'), applied to "a/an."

### EXPLAIN
- **Masculine:** UN (default — before any consonant AND before vowels, never an apostrophe) vs. UNO (before S+consonant, Z, GN, PS, X — same trigger set as "lo").
- **Feminine:** UNA (default — before any consonant, including S+consonant/Z/GN; no separate form for those) vs. UN' (apostrophe, before any vowel).
- Explicitly flagged the asymmetry: masculine has 2 forms triggered by consonant-type; feminine has 2 forms triggered by vowel-vs-consonant only, with no "uno"-equivalent.

### CHECK
1. "un orologio" or "un'orologio"? → **un orologio** (correct, masculine never takes apostrophe)
2. "una amica" or "un'amica"? → **un'amica** (correct)

### PRACTICE (unaided, 4 items)
zaino→uno, studente→uno, isola→un', gatto→un — **4/4 correct**

### REFLECT
Asked why "uno" exists separately and why feminine has no equivalent.
- First attempt: said "uno" reflects both "lo e l'" — inaccurate, "uno" only mirrors lo's trigger set (l' triggers vowel/H and does NOT produce a masculine indefinite apostrophe form). Also first explanation for the feminine asymmetry ("não existe esse gatilho") was reasoning from absence rather than mechanism.
- Follow-up question asked specifically why it's the consonant-ending of "un" (not gender itself) that creates the need for "uno".
- Second attempt: "pela fluência fonética" — accepted as correct and complete.
