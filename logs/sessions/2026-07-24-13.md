## Topic taught: Avere (to have) — present tense

- WHY: avere is the second most-used verb after essere, needed for possession, age, idiomatic expressions (ho fame, avete ragione), and later for passato prossimo (Stage 03).
- EXPLAIN: full table taught in one pass — ho, hai, ha, abbiamo, avete, hanno — including the silent-H spelling rule upfront (H is written but never pronounced; exists to distinguish homophones like hanno/anno, ho/o).
- SHOW: saved worked examples to `playground/avere-examples.md` (io ho una macchina, tu hai vent'anni?, lui ha fame, noi abbiamo tempo, voi avete ragione, loro hanno due gatti).
- CHECK 1: translate "Eu tenho vinte anos" → learner answered "io ho vent'anni" — correct, unaided, Good.
- CHECK 2: complete "Loro ___ fame" → learner answered "lor hanno fame" — conjugation correct; "loro" missing final -o was a typo (confirmed by learner), not a concept gap. Good.
- PRACTICE: translate "Nós temos razão" → learner wrote "noi abbiamo razione" — conjugation ("abbiamo") correct, but "ragione" misspelled. Learner then correctly objected that "ragione" was never taught as vocabulary (only appeared once, unflagged, in the SHOW example) — valid pushback, saved as new feedback memory (`feedback_vocab_in_practice`). Final answer "noi abbiamo ragione" confirmed correct, Good.
- REFLECT (Feynman): asked why the silent H exists in ho/hai/ha/hanno. Learner answered: "para diferenciar de outras palavras, como por exemplo diferenciar hanno de anno (ano)" — fully correct, unaided, no gaps.
